# Product Design & UX

**Phase**: Cuts across all phases  
**Audience**: The nervous first-tune owner

---

## Target User

Someone who just got a remote tune flashed via MHD or BM3. They paid a tuner they've never met, were told to "pull some logs and check in," and are now staring at a CSV they don't fully understand.

**What they need from this tool**:
- **Validation**: Is my car okay?
- **Education**: What does this finding mean?
- **Guidance**: What should I do next?
- **A bridge**: Language to communicate with their tuner

**What the tool must NOT do**: Replace the tuner. Every result should reinforce the tuner relationship, not bypass it.

---

## Three Severity Lanes

Every finding must land in exactly one of three lanes. This is the core output contract.

| Lane | Label | When to use | Visual treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stop — contact your tuner now** | Knock, dangerous lean condition | Red, prominent, unmissable |
| 2 | **Mention to your tuner** | Minor timing pull, throttle closure, borderline readings | Yellow/amber |
| 3 | **Normal and expected** | WGDC ceiling on stock turbo, healthy AFR readings | Green, brief |

The current Streamlit output does not distinguish these clearly enough. A first-tune owner should be able to read the output without knowing what WGDC means and still understand whether to call their tuner.

**Implementation**: Each `Alert` object (see [engine.md](engine.md)) maps to a severity lane. The overall report also has a top-level lane — determined by the worst finding. If any CRITICAL alert exists, the report is lane 1.

---

## The Clean Bill of Health Path

Most logs from a well-tuned car will be fine. The tool must treat this as the primary success case — not an absence of alerts, but an explicit, warm confirmation.

**Target output for a clean log**:
> "Your car is running exactly as your tuner intended. Boost is on target, fuel pressure is stable, no knock detected. You're good."

This is the output the nervous user came for. A blank alert list is not the same thing — the user doesn't know if blank means healthy or broken. Make healthy explicit.

**Implementation**: Add a `clean_bill_of_health` boolean to the result. When `True` and no alerts exist, render the clean bill of health message as the hero output — not a sidebar or footnote.

---

## "What to Send Your Tuner" Section

Every result, including clean logs, should include a pre-written tuner message the user can copy directly.

**For a clean log**:
> "Hey, ran a log today. Everything looks clean — no knock, fuel pressure stable, boost on target. Just wanted to check in."

**For a finding**:
> "Hey, ran a log today. The analyzer flagged a timing pull of -4.2° on Cyl 3. Everything else looks clean. Is this within normal range for this map?"

**For a critical finding**:
> "Hey, I need to talk before my next drive. The analyzer flagged what looks like knock on Cyl 3. I haven't driven it since. Can we talk?"

**Implementation**: Generated by a template renderer in Phase 2 using the structured findings from the rule engine.

---

## Shareable Results URL

The URL `/result/{job_id}` is a shareable link to the diagnosis. Surface this explicitly in the UI.

**Copy link button placement**: Immediately below the overall score, before the findings. This is the first thing a user will want to do — share with their tuner.

**UI text**: "Share this link with your tuner — it will work for 24 hours."

**On direct load** (user navigates to a `/result/{id}` URL):
- Skip the upload screen
- Poll DynamoDB for the result directly
- If the item has expired (TTL deleted it): show "This result has expired. Upload a new log to get a fresh analysis."

---

## Onboarding: How to Pull Your First Log

"Upload CSV Log" as the entry point assumes knowledge the target user may not have. Include a collapsible "How to pull your first log" section on the upload screen.

**MHD guide**:
1. Enable logging parameters: RPM, boost pressure (absolute + target), AFR, rail pressure, ignition timing (per-cylinder + correction), intake air temp, throttle position
2. How to do a safe WOT pull (3rd gear, 30-60 mph, wide open throttle to redline)
3. How to export the CSV from the MHD app

**BM3 guide**:
1. Enable equivalent parameters in BM3 logging
2. Same WOT pull instructions
3. Export from BM3

**UX**: Collapsible — not shown by default, doesn't intimidate users who already know how. Link opens it. Keep it short — 5-7 bullet points per platform, no paragraphs.

## Future UX (Post-MVP)

- **Multi-pull comparison view**: Show pull 1 vs pull 2 vs pull 3 side-by-side. Heat soak is immediately visible. Currently discarded — see [engine.md](engine.md).
- **Tune-stage context**: "On a Stage 2 E30 tune, this WGDC reading is actually expected." Context-aware explanations require `tune_stage` to be stored (already planned for DynamoDB schema).
- **Log history**: Requires user accounts. Would let a user track their car's health over time across multiple logs.
